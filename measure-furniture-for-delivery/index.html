FURNITURE FIT MEASURING GUIDE

How to measure if furniture will fit

Measure the furniture, every narrow point on the delivery route, and the usable destination space. This step-by-step method helps you work out whether a sofa, couch, table, dresser, wardrobe or other large furniture can fit before delivery.

How to measure furniture to see if it will fit

Use the maximum fixed dimensions of the item as it will be delivered. Measure from the furthest point to the furthest point rather than only the main body. Arms, frames, handles, headboards, fixed legs and trim can be the dimensions that decide whether an item clears a doorway.

If the seller states that a component is removable for delivery, keep two sets of measurements: the normal assembled size and the approved delivery configuration. Never assume a structural part can be removed just because doing so would make the numbers work.

Width or length

Record the longest horizontal side. For a sofa, this is usually arm-to-arm. For a table or dresser, measure the widest fixed points. For a bed frame or wardrobe, measure the largest rigid component that will actually travel through the route.

Height

Measure from the lowest delivered point to the highest fixed point. If the item may travel on its side, height can become one side of the doorway cross-section.

Depth

Measure front to back at the deepest fixed points. Depth often becomes important when furniture is carried end-first through a doorway.

Measure the route, not just the destination

The narrowest or most awkward point determines delivery access. Walk from the unloading point to the final room and note every gate, entrance, hallway, internal door, stair, landing and elevator. For each opening, measure the clear usable space rather than the advertised or nominal size.

Doorways

Measure clear width and clear height with the door fully open. Check door stops, frames, handles and thresholds. If the furniture must turn immediately after the door, also measure the free space in front of the opening. See the doorway measuring guide.

Hallways and corners

Measure the hallway width at its narrowest point and the space at any 90-degree turn. A long item can clear the front door but still fail because it cannot rotate around the next corner.

Stairs and landings

Record the clear stair width, overhead clearance, rail position and landing size. Look for sloped ceilings or low points that may not be obvious from the bottom of the stairs.

Elevators

Measure the elevator door opening as well as the interior width, depth and height. The item must both enter the elevator and fit inside it in a workable orientation.

Measure the destination room

Record the usable floor length and width, not only wall-to-wall dimensions. Radiators, built-ins, fireplaces, door swings and existing furniture can reduce the practical space. A piece can technically fit inside the room but still block a walkway or prevent a door or drawer from opening.

Tip: Mark the furniture footprint on the floor with painter’s tape, cardboard or paper. This does not prove delivery access, but it helps confirm whether the final layout feels usable.

Different furniture types: what matters most?

Sofas and couches

Measure arms, frame height and depth. Test the largest delivered module if the sofa separates. Use the sofa fit through door guide for a focused walkthrough.

Tables and desks

Confirm whether legs or bases are removable. If not, measure the widest fixed frame and the full depth underneath protruding supports.

Dressers and wardrobes

These pieces are often rigid, so maximum width, height and depth matter. Check whether doors, drawers or shelves are removed for transport, but only use reduced measurements when that is the actual delivery configuration.

Bed frames and mattresses

Test the largest rigid bed component separately. Mattresses can flex to different degrees, so a rigid-box calculator is only an initial screen for a mattress; follow the manufacturer’s handling guidance.
