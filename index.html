How do I know if furniture will fit through a door?

Measure the furniture at its widest, tallest and deepest fixed points, then measure the doorway’s clear opening. The calculator checks three rectangular faces of the item across rotated angles from 0° to 90°, then separately checks the furniture footprint against the room if you enter room dimensions.

This is a screening tool, not a delivery guarantee. Tight corners, stairs, packaging, flexible cushions and the space needed to pivot can change the real-world result.

STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE

How to measure a room and doorway for furniture

The safest check covers both the destination and the entire delivery route. A sofa can fit comfortably inside a living room and still fail at the front door or a tight turn just beyond it.

1. Measure the furniture at its maximum points

Record width or length, height and depth. Include fixed arms, frames, headboards, handles or other parts that cannot be removed for delivery. If the manufacturer confirms that legs or another component can be removed safely, record the reduced dimensions separately and run a second check.

2. Measure the clear doorway opening

Measure the actual usable gap when the door is fully open. Frames, stops, handles and thresholds can reduce the space available. Measure the narrowest clear width and usable height rather than relying on a nominal door size.

3. Check more than one orientation

A sofa or other piece may not pass straight through but may clear the opening on its side or after being rotated. This calculator tests the three rectangular faces formed by the item’s width, height and depth, then samples rotated angles through the doorway opening.

What the calculator does not model: a full 3D pivot around a corner, stair rails, flexible compression, protective packaging, removable doors, uneven floors or the thickness of a deep opening. Those need a physical route check.

4. Follow the complete delivery route

Measure every narrow point from outside to the final room: gates, entrance doors, hallways, internal doors, stairs, landings and elevators. Also check the space immediately before and after a doorway because the item may need room to rotate.

5. Check the destination room

The optional room fields test whether the furniture’s width-by-depth footprint can fit inside the usable room dimensions in normal or rotated orientation. A technical footprint fit does not automatically mean the layout is comfortable, so still account for walkways, other furniture and door swings.

How this furniture fit calculator works

For each of the three possible rectangular faces of your item, the calculator checks orientations between 0° and 90°. At each angle it calculates the face’s horizontal and vertical bounding dimensions and compares them with the doorway after your safety margin is applied. This is more useful than comparing only one straight orientation, while remaining a conservative screening method rather than a full moving simulation.

Will a sofa or couch fit through my doorway?

Start with the sofa’s overall width, height and depth and the doorway’s clear width and height. A sofa may enter with its depth leading, on its side, or in another rotated orientation. If the calculator reports a possible doorway fit, you still need enough space on both sides of the opening to perform that movement. For a more focused walkthrough, use the sofa fit through door guide and calculator.

Common furniture measuring mistakes

  • Measuring the room but forgetting the route into the room.
  • Using advertised dimensions without checking the widest fixed points.
  • Measuring the door slab instead of the clear usable opening.
  • Ignoring handles, frames, stair rails, thresholds and tight turns.
  • Assuming a zero-clearance result means movers can actually maneuver the item.
  • Forgetting that packaging can be larger than the product itself.

Before delivery: a 60-second checklist

  1. Write down furniture width, height and depth.
  2. Measure the narrowest clear doorway width and height.
  3. Walk the full route and identify the tightest turn.
  4. Check stairs, landings and elevator dimensions where relevant.
  5. Measure the usable destination room.
  6. Confirm with the seller which parts can be removed safely.

FAQ

Furniture fit and measuring questions

What measurements do I need before buying a sofa?

Record the sofa’s maximum width or length, height and depth, plus the clear width and height of every doorway on the route. If you want to check the destination too, measure the usable room length and width.

Can a couch fit through a door if it is wider than the door?

Sometimes. A couch may pass with another dimension leading through the opening or after rotation. That is why the calculator tests different rectangular faces and angles rather than comparing only the couch’s overall width with the door width.

Does this calculator guarantee my furniture will fit?

No. It is a geometric screening tool. Tight turns, stairs, landing space, packaging, removable parts and irregular or flexible shapes can change the real-world result.

Should I measure furniture with the legs attached?

Measure the item as it will actually be delivered. If the manufacturer confirms that legs can be removed safely, you can also test the reduced dimensions separately.

Should I measure the door or the doorway?

Measure the clear usable opening the furniture must pass through. The door slab’s advertised size is not the same thing as the space available once frames, stops and hardware are considered.
